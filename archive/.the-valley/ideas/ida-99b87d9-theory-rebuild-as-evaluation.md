# Theory rebuilding is an evaluation — of models and of the graph

Rebuilding the theory from the graph alone is a measurable exercise, and it measures two things at
once: a mind's capability and the graph's adequacy. Held against a fixed graph, varying the model
gives a capability ladder — which rung of model suffices for which class of work stops being
intuition. Held against a fixed model as the graph evolves, it tracks the graph's own revival cost
over time.

The ultimate arbiter is the theory holder: whether a mind has understood is a judgment, not a score
a check computes. But the strongest models that have convinced the theory holder of their
understanding can approximate that judgment for lower rungs of capability — calibrated judges
scoring what the holder need not personally re-judge. A human judgment anchors the top of the chain
and mechanical approximation extends it downward, the same shape the rest of the system gives to
trust.

Probe shapes, deliberately small: answer why-questions; propose a modification and defend its fit;
detect a plausible change that violates the theory.
