usage: valley rebase [--known-signers <file>] [--integrator-name <name>] <change>

  rebase [--known-signers <file>] [--integrator-name <name>] <change>
                   rebase the change's whole stack onto origin/main, as
                   review's [b]ase onto main does: the layers pending
                   lists under the change, and the change itself. in a
                   throwaway worktree, bottom first, each layer's own commits
                   go onto the rebased layer below it, and the bottom's onto
                   origin/main, so each layer keeps its own diff. commits
                   under the bottom that a lower change landed through the
                   integrator drop out, since main holds them; that landing
                   is read as changes reads it, with the signers its two
                   flags, VALLEY_KNOWN_SIGNERS and VALLEY_INTEGRATOR_SIGNER,
                   or the host's publication give. one atomic
                   push then moves every layer's branch, each leased on the
                   tip read: every branch moves, or none does. last, bottom
                   first, each change gains its rebased head as a new
                   version, as valley.principal names, the way ready
                   --if-active does: only while the change is still ready
                   at the head the rebase began with.
                   it refuses, with nothing changed, a stack holding a
                   change that is not ready, records no branch, or whose
                   branch is missing on origin or is not at its recorded
                   head; a plain branch that is the branch of a change not
                   in the stack; two layers on one branch; a merge among the
                   commits to be rebased, or a version head of a change
                   that is not ready or not in the stack, since a rebase
                   gives each commit a new id; and a stack holding a change
                   while git config valley.principal names no one. a
                   conflict in any layer pushes nothing, records nothing
                   and names the layer.
                   a change alone prints its new head. a
                   stack prints a manifest: one line for each layer, with
                   its old and new head, whether its branch was pushed, and
                   whether a version was added. then, for each change left
                   unfinished, the valley ready command that finishes it,
                   and the exit status is non-zero. rebasing again refuses
                   a change whose branch holds a head no version records,
                   and prints the same command. last, it names each ready
                   change or branch outside the stack that is left on an
                   old head of a layer, such as a second stack on a lower
                   layer, with the rebase that moves it; it does not move
                   it. rebase does not re-review the result. a stack that
                   already fast-forwards from main is left as it is.
