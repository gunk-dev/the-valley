usage: valley telemetry [--remote <remote>] [--json] <operation> [flags]

  sources                 every source the host declares, and its state:
                          disabled, unavailable, failed, stale, active or
                          idle, with its units, its records and how far its
                          journal reaches
  snapshot                sources, with each producer's exported state and
                          its open and latest runs
  read [--source <name>] [--since <cursor>] [--limit <n>] [--logs]
                          records after the cursor (default: the newest
                          --limit, 200, at most 1000); a source's systemd
                          lifecycle from the journal; with --logs, its own
                          output and the tail of its build logs, marked
                          untrusted
  follow [--source <name>] [--since <cursor>] [--seconds <n>] [--logs]
                          records as they are stored, and with --logs build
                          output as it is written, for --seconds (default
                          60, at most 300); ends with a cursor to resume from
  flush                   send this workstation's spooled records now

Reading needs a key whose principal holds the host's observe grant. The
remote is the checkout's valley.telemetry.remote, else origin, or the one
--remote names (a remote, an ssh URL, or user@host:path); its URL is read
and never run. --json prints the valley-telemetry/v1 answer lines as they
come. A gap, a cut-short answer or an error is said; an answer that does
not end exits 1.

With `git config valley.telemetry true`, every valley command records a
command record: its verb (and knot's or telemetry's subverb), the names of
the flags it was given, its project, its exit status and its wall time,
and for pending and status the milliseconds and processes each phase took.
It never records an argument's value, a path, a title or a body. Every
approval attempt, from approve or from review, also records its steps and
its checks' progress. Records go to an owner-only spool for the
destination the checkout's telemetry remote names, under
$XDG_STATE_HOME/valley/telemetry/spools. A background flusher sends each
spool to its own destination, with the report grant; flush does the same
now, and --remote limits it to that remote's spool. Telemetry never fails
or holds up a command, and changes nothing it prints.
